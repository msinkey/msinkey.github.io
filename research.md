## Recent Work

**Alcohol Consumption and Violent Crime Prevalence: Evidence
from Texas Counties**, with Bruce Bird, Hilde Patron and Joey Smith ([PDF](/files/Texas_Alcohol_Project-9.pdf))

*Abstract:* The channels by which alcohol consumption may contribute to violent crime are diverse. In
this study, we focus on the effects of alcohol consumption on the categories of reported rape
and reported aggravated assault, as defined by data collected by local police jurisdictions in
Texas. We first provide causal estimates that examine how expanded access to alcohol affects
alcohol sales and purchase locations. Utilizing a 14-year panel of Texas counties, we document,
via recent difference-in-differences methodology, that removal of “by-the-drink” and “by-the-bottle”
purchase restrictions led to large, economically and statistically significant increases in
the number of bars, restaurants, and outlets where alcohol could be purchased. In particular,
we estimate that removal of by-the-drink restrictions led to between $18,000 and $50,000 of
alcohol sales per county and an average of one to three liquor licenses per county, and that
removal of by-the-bottle restrictions led to between 1.2 and 1.87 million in by-the-bottle sales,
with an increase of between 1.9 to 3.52 outlets. Despite these large, positive increases in outlets
and sales in these counties, using the same causal methodology, we find little to no evidence
for any additional increase in the categories of reported rapes or reported aggravated assaults.
Moreover, we do not find evidence to suggest that policy changes in border counties cause any
additional increase in crime across counties.
